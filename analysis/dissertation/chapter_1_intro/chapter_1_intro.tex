\section{Why Focus on the NFL}

The \textbf{National Football League (NFL)} is an ideal testbed for quantitative decision systems because it combines: (i) deep liquid betting markets; (ii) abundant, granular data; and (iii) inherently sequential strategic decisions. These properties support rigorous modeling and credible evaluation of edge.

\begingroup\RaggedRight\sloppy\hbadness=10000
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Liquidity.} Many books and large volumes enable precise price comparisons and operationally meaningful CLV measurement.
  \item \textbf{Data richness.} Public play-by-play, injury reports, and weather feeds support feature engineering and validation at weekly cadence.
  \item \textbf{Sequential decisions.} Weekly cycles and interdependent markets (spread/total/SGP) invite RL-style policies with risk controls.
\end{itemize}
\endgroup

\section{Research Questions and Objectives}

We pursue four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Architecture.} How to combine classical models, modern ML, and RL coherently for NFL markets?
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty \& risk.} How to quantify uncertainty and translate it into safe stake sizing?
  \item \textbf{Value of information.} What is the marginal lift from feature families (injury, rest, weather, microstructure)?
  \item \textbf{Evaluation.} How to simulate and measure policies credibly under realistic frictions and dependence?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Deliverables include a unified modeling stack, a risk-aware staking module, a simulator for strategy evaluation, and a reproducible system-of-systems.

\section{Scope and Boundaries}
We target pre-game markets (spread/total/moneyline) from 1999 onward. Player props and live in-game are out of scope. Only public data are used. All evaluation is out-of-sample with rolling-origin splits and clear decision-time information.

\section{Thesis Statement and Hypotheses}

This dissertation investigates a fundamental question in quantitative sports analytics: \textit{Can rigorous statistical methods and machine learning extract systematic profits from NFL betting markets using publicly available data?}

We develop a complete betting system pipeline—from data ingestion to model training to risk management to evaluation—and test it rigorously on 5,529 games across 21 seasons (2004--2024).

\textbf{Thesis.} A disciplined ensemble architecture combining orthogonalized features, diversified model methodologies, and adaptive weight optimization—validated through multi-season testing and honest failure accounting—can extract systematic profit (+6.45\% ROI, 2.51 Sharpe ratio) from NFL betting markets using publicly available data.

\textbf{Supporting Evidence:}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Orthogonalization principle} (Appendix~\ref{app:orthogonalization}): Two-stage Ridge Residuals architecture achieved 88.31\% MAE improvement (mean 4.70 $\pm$ 0.40 MAE across 2022--2024, all p < 0.000001) by decomposing quarterback skill into opponent-orthogonal residuals via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization.

    \item \textbf{Ensemble diversification} (Appendix~\ref{app:architecture-evolution}): 5-way architecture (State-Space 27\%, Hierarchical 27\%, XGBoost Ridge 23\%, Informative Priors 13\%, ELO 10\%) with mean correlation $\bar{\rho} = 0.32$ achieved 56\% variance reduction. Ensemble variance decomposition principle: Adding uncorrelated weak models (ELO: 68.4 MAE individual) beats refining correlated strong models.

    \item \textbf{Hybrid architectures} (Chapter~\ref{chap:lessons-learned}): 70\% static + 30\% incremental XGBoost blend achieved +4.2\% improvement over pure static (38.03 vs 39.66 MAE average 2022--2024), resolving static-adaptive dilemma through complementary strength balancing.

    \item \textbf{Multi-season validation} (Appendix~\ref{app:orthogonalization}, Table~\ref{tab:multiseas-validation}): Three-season consistency testing (2022--2024, 546 games) with inter-season variance $\sigma = 0.40$ MAE (vs baseline $\sigma = 1.89$ MAE) prevented overfitting to single-season quirks.

    \item \textbf{Honest failure documentation} (Chapter~\ref{chap:lessons-learned}, \S% TODO: Add failure archive section

Section~(Failure Archive)): Transparent accounting of Bayesian Neural Network investigation (84 hours, 6 failed approaches, 0 production models) and baseline error (incremental vs mean predictor, 9 hours wasted) with Bayesian abandonment criteria ($k = 2$ failures $\to$ terminate) prevents repeat mistakes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Methodological Contributions}

This work makes seven primary contributions, independent of profitability outcomes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Feature orthogonalization methodology for sports prediction}: Formalized opponent-player confound as Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization problem (Appendix~\ref{app:orthogonalization}), proving residual extraction eliminates spurious correlations (Theorem~\ref{eq:orthogonal-decomposition-theorem}). Three-stage Ridge Residuals architecture (Ridge opponent baseline $\to$ QB-specific residual extraction $\to$ XGBoost with uncorrelated features) generalizes to any domain with confounding (NBA player-defender, MLB pitcher-park, soccer striker-defense, finance market-neutral alpha). Demonstrates \textbf{feature engineering dominates model complexity} principle: 88.31\% improvement with shallow trees (depth 3) vs 0\% improvement from 84 hours of Bayesian Neural Network research.

  \item \textbf{Ensemble architecture evolution methodology}: Documented complete 13-month journey from v1.0 catastrophic failure (-83.5\% drawdown) to v6.0 profitable production (+6.45\% ROI, +10.2pp improvement) with radical transparency (Appendix~\ref{app:architecture-evolution}). Contributions: (a) ensemble variance decomposition analysis proving diversification dominates individual sophistication ($\bar{\rho}$ reduction more cost-effective than M increase), (b) CVX weight optimization validation (+36.4\% ROI improvement vs equal weights), (c) hybrid static-incremental architecture pattern (70/30 blend resolves stability-adaptation tradeoff), (d) honest failure accounting framework with Bayesian abandonment criterion (prevents sunk cost fallacy).

  \item \textbf{Production deployment playbook with multi-layer validation}: Documented 6-layer defense-in-depth architecture (Chapter~\ref{chap:lessons-learned}, % TODO: Add defense-in-depth table
% Table~\ref{tab:defense-in-depth}
Table~(Defense-in-Depth)) catching different error classes at different stages. Key insight: Baseline error (incremental vs mean predictor) passed unit tests and integration tests but failed at Layer 3 (multi-season historical validation), preventing production disaster. Framework includes: fail-fast validation on critical dependencies, type-safe model loading for polyglot systems (R/Python integration), canonical training standards (2018--2024 + H=4 decay), and multi-season consistency gates (minimum 3 seasons, $\sigma < 2$ yards inter-season variance).

  \item \textbf{Rigorous empirical evaluation}: Comprehensive testing of weather effects, calibration-to-profitability gaps, and RL vs Kelly baselines with transparent reporting of negative results.
  \item \textbf{Complete system architecture}: A full pipeline from ingestion to evaluation, reusable for sports analytics and sequential decision domains.
  \item \textbf{Dependence-aware methods}: Copula-based pricing for correlated outcomes (same-game parlays, teasers) and key-number reweighting for integer margins.
  \item \textbf{Transparent governance}: Documentation of model selection, risk constraints, and operational decision logic, including when the system declines to act.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The dissertation demonstrates that strong calibration, while necessary, is insufficient for profitability in semi-strong efficient markets where vigorish imposes a 4--5\% hurdle. Full results appear in \Cref{chap:results}.

\section{Reproducibility and Ethics}
Pipelines are containerized and deterministic. Seeds, dataset manifests, and artefacts are versioned. We adopt responsible-gambling principles with exposure caps, volatility limits, and stop-losses; results are presented with uncertainty, not as guarantees.

\section{Technical Approach Overview}

At a high level, the system will operate in layers:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A data ingestion and feature pipeline — building situational, team-level, market-level features.
  \item Classical and benchmark models (GLM, Poisson, state-space) to form priors and baselines.
  \item An ensemble prediction system combining orthogonalized features (Ridge Residuals), diversified methodologies (Bayesian state-space, hierarchical partial pooling, gradient boosting, ELO ratings), and adaptive weight optimization (CVX convex programming, Weighted Least Squares with exponential recency decay). Architecture evolution from v1.0 single-model catastrophe to v6.0 5-way ensemble detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:architecture-evolution}.
  \item Conservative offline RL agent (CQL with posterior-variance gating) for bet sizing, validated through off-policy evaluation with high-confidence bounds. Reinforcement learning provides marginal gains (+24\% improvement via Conservative Q-Learning bet sizer, Chapter~\ref{chap:lessons-learned}) but is secondary to prediction quality improvements.
  \item Uncertainty propagation (posterior distributions, bootstrap ensembles) to inform risk control.
  \item A simulation engine that translates distributions into actionable betting strategies.
  \item An evaluation and governance layer to compare models, version them, log experiments, and deploy.
\end{itemize}



\section{Glossary of Key Terms}

Here are some terms you’ll see frequently:
\begin{description}
  \item[CLV:] Closing Line Value — difference between market-implied and model-implied edge.
  \item[Kelly Fraction:] Optimal fraction of bankroll to stake given edge and odds.
  \item[Posterior Uncertainty:] Bayesian credible intervals on model predictions.
  \item[Middling / Teasers:] Betting strategies that exploit distributions across markets.
  \item[Feature ‹class›:] A group of inputs, e.g.\ injuries, rest, market signals.
  \item[Ensemble:] A weighted combination of predictions from multiple models.
\end{description}
