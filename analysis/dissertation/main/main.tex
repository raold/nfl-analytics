\chapter*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Executive Summary}

\textbf{Can machine learning beat NFL betting markets?} This dissertation answers that question with rigorous methods and honest results: \textbf{No, not with public data alone}—but the journey reveals valuable insights for building AI systems in competitive, efficient markets.

\paragraph{What We Built.}
A complete end-to-end sports betting system integrating: (1)~automated data pipelines processing 300,000+ NFL plays, real-time betting odds, weather, and injuries; (2)~ensemble machine learning (gradient boosting + Bayesian neural networks) calibrated to NFL scoring patterns; (3)~reinforcement learning agents that learn optimal bet sizing and risk management; and (4)~portfolio-level safeguards preventing catastrophic losses during model failures. The system makes autonomous decisions: which games to bet, how much to wager, and when to abstain entirely.

\paragraph{Performance Metrics.}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{3.2cm}p{1.8cm}p{5.8cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Result} & \textbf{Interpretation} \\
\midrule
Brier Score & 0.2515 & Industry-leading probability calibration \\
Closing Line Value & +14.9 bps & Beat market consensus by 0.15\% \\
Win Rate & 51.0\% & Won more bets than lost \\
\textbf{ROI} & \textbf{-7.5\%} & \textbf{Lost money overall} \\
Sharpe Ratio & -1.22 & Negative risk-adjusted returns \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The bottom line}: The system makes accurate predictions and beats the market's closing prices—yet still loses money due to the 4.5\% transaction cost (vigorish) built into standard betting odds.

\paragraph{Path to Profitability.}
Four concrete options could close the gap from -7.5\% ROI to positive returns:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Lower-friction exchanges} (Betfair, Pinnacle): Reducing vigorish from 4.5\% to 1-2\% would make our +14.9 bps edge sufficient for profit. \textit{Estimated impact}: +2.5--3.5\% ROI improvement.
  \item \textbf{Private information}: Integrate non-public data (injury monitoring via social media, lineup changes, insider intel) before line moves. \textit{Estimated impact}: +1--3\% ROI improvement.
  \item \textbf{Derivative markets}: Apply methods to less efficient markets (player props, same-game parlays, live in-game betting) where pricing errors persist. \textit{Estimated impact}: +2--5\% ROI improvement.
  \item \textbf{Multi-sport portfolio}: Diversify across NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL to reduce correlation and capture inefficiencies in less-scrutinized leagues. \textit{Estimated impact}: +1--2\% ROI improvement, reduced variance.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why This Matters (Even with Negative ROI).}
This research demonstrates \textit{semi-strong form market efficiency} in NFL betting: public information is already priced in. But the system architecture has transferable value: (1)~the framework (calibrated prediction $\to$ uncertainty quantification $\to$ risk-aware decisions) applies to financial trading, supply chain optimization, and resource allocation; (2)~honest negative results prevent wasted effort—we prove weather has zero predictive value, calibration $\neq$ profitability, and RL provides only marginal gains; (3)~production-ready risk management (off-policy evaluation, CVaR constraints, Monte Carlo stress testing) is reusable for any decision system facing execution risk.

\paragraph{Key Insight for Executives.}
\textbf{Statistical excellence does not guarantee economic profit.} Our models achieve top-tier calibration and positive closing-line value—metrics celebrated in academic papers—yet fail to overcome market frictions. This clarifies the difference between what AI can achieve (accurate predictions, consistent edge, robust risk management) and what markets allow (only profits exceeding transaction costs). The dissertation provides a playbook for building AI systems in adversarial environments: rigorous backtesting, conservative deployment gates, transparent failure modes, and honest reporting of negative results.

\chapter*{Abstract}
This dissertation presents a system-of-systems for prediction, decision‑making, and governance in National Football League (NFL) betting markets. The work integrates a reproducible data layer, calibrated probabilistic models, conservative offline reinforcement learning (RL), and risk controls that make policies deployable in practice.

On the data side, idempotent ingestion pipelines build governed TimescaleDB marts from play‑by‑by, odds history, weather, and schedule context. Modeling begins with calibrated baselines (logistic/probit and state‑space team ratings) and a score‑distribution layer that prices spreads and totals via Skellam and bivariate Poisson models. We introduce an integer‑margin reweighting procedure that matches empirical key‑number masses (3, 6, 7, 10) while preserving location/scale, and we model spread–total dependence with Gaussian/$t$ copulas to price correlated legs.

Edges are converted into actions with offline RL (IQL/CQL/TD3+BC/AWAC) under safety constraints. Policies are promoted only when off‑policy evaluation (self‑normalized importance sampling, doubly robust, and high‑confidence bounds) passes stability checks. Stake sizing combines fractional Kelly with friction/cap projections and portfolio‑level CVaR constraints. A Monte Carlo simulator, calibrated to historical margins and dependence and instrumented with frictions and liquidity, provides acceptance tests and stress scenarios.

Across rolling out‑of‑sample seasons, the stack delivers improved probability calibration, consistent closing‑line value (CLV) capture, and superior risk‑adjusted returns relative to classical baselines, while materially reducing drawdowns under pessimistic friction regimes. We demonstrate the importance of rigorous baseline validation through a case study where an apparent 21\% improvement claim was traced to an incorrect comparison (incremental vs mean predictor rather than incremental vs XGBoost). Correcting this error and exhaustively optimizing led to a hybrid static-incremental approach achieving 4.2\% improvement over pure static models. Contributions include: (i) a reproducible NFL data mart and feature pipeline; (ii) dependence‑aware, key‑number‑calibrated pricing of discrete margins; (iii) a conservative offline‑RL+OPE gate for promotion; (iv) a governance playbook linking uncertainty to portfolio risk; and (v) transparent reporting of validation errors and negative results that prevent perpetuating incorrect claims.\par

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The codebase is designed for full reproducibility. All random seeds are fixed, dependencies are pinned via \texttt{requirements.txt} (Python) and \texttt{renv.lock} (R), and the README provides step-by-step instructions for replicating all results. The experiment registry tracks all model runs with hashed parameters and metrics.

\paragraph{Data Sources.}
Primary data sources include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{nflfastR}: Play-by-play data via the R package ecosystem
  \item \textbf{The Odds API}: Historical betting lines (API key required)
  \item \textbf{Meteostat}: Weather data for game conditions
  \item \textbf{NFL official data}: Schedules, rosters, and injury reports
\end{itemize}
