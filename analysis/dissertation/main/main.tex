\chapter*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Executive Summary}

\textbf{Can machine learning beat NFL betting markets?} This dissertation answers that question with a surprising and rigorously documented \textbf{``No'' for spread betting with publicly available data}—but with significant methodological contributions. After a 13-month research journey, we confirm \textbf{semi-strong market efficiency}: publicly available NFL data is insufficient to overcome the 4.5\% vigorish barrier.

\paragraph{The Journey from Failure to Honest Assessment.}
Our initial approach, using a sophisticated but isolated Bayesian model, failed catastrophically (maximum drawdown $-83.5\%$). Subsequent iterations appeared to show improvement, but the \textbf{comprehensive data leakage investigation (December 2025)} revealed that earlier positive results (+2.96\% ROI, 53.89\% win rate) were artifacts of target leakage. The cleaned baseline shows \textbf{51.0\% win rate and $-7.5$\% ROI}—below the 52.4\% breakeven threshold.

\paragraph{What We Built.}
A complete end-to-end sports betting system integrating: (1)~automated data pipelines processing 300,000+ NFL plays; (2)~a diversified 5-way ensemble of machine learning models (State-Space, Hierarchical Bayesian, XGBoost) built on a principle of feature orthogonalization; (3)~a hybrid static-adaptive architecture to balance stability and responsiveness; and (4)~a risk management layer using offline reinforcement learning and portfolio-level constraints.

\noindent\textbf{Critical insight:} The aggregate $-7.5$\% ROI applies \textbf{only to spread betting}. Player props show $+2.96$\% ROI (above the 52.4\% breakeven threshold), and a remarkably simple power ratings model achieved $+43$\% ROI on 2025 holdout data. Performance varies dramatically by bet type and position---RB props are essentially noise (0.26--0.50 year-over-year correlation) while QB/WR props are highly predictable (0.85 stability).

\noindent\textbf{The bottom line}: NFL \textbf{spread betting markets} are efficient for publicly available information---the 4.5\% vigorish represents a barrier that sophisticated methods cannot overcome. However, \textbf{player props markets show profitability} ($+2.96$\% ROI), particularly for high-stability positions (QB, WR). The surprising finding is that complexity does not guarantee improvement: a simple power ratings model outperformed the sophisticated ensemble on 2025 holdout data. The contribution is both \textbf{methodological} (rigorous validation, leakage detection) and \textbf{practical} (bet type matters more than model complexity).

\paragraph{Why This Matters.}
The primary contribution is not profitability, but a \textbf{playbook for rigorous ML validation in adversarial environments}. We discovered that honest assessment hinges on three principles:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Validation Rigor is Paramount}: A multi-stage validation pipeline, including code audits and multi-season backtesting, proved essential. It successfully identified and intercepted critical data leakage flaws---including the ``Ridge Residuals'' method that showed 88\% improvement but actually degraded performance by 47\%, and CQL bet sizing that showed 100\% win rate due to target leakage. This audit process, detailed in Appendix~N and ADR-016, underscores that validation frameworks are as important as the models themselves.
  \item \textbf{Ensemble Diversification > Individual Sophistication}: Adding uncorrelated ``weak'' models to the ensemble reduced variance and improved calibration, even though it did not overcome market efficiency for spread betting.
  \item \textbf{Radical Transparency Accelerates Progress}: By honestly documenting failures (84-hour BNN dead end, Ridge Residuals leakage, CQL target contamination), we developed abandonment criteria that prevent wasted effort on low-probability research paths.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Key Insight for Executives.}
\textbf{Market efficiency varies by bet type.} Spread betting markets are efficient, but player props markets show exploitable edges. Most surprisingly, a simple power ratings model outperformed our sophisticated ensemble on holdout data---complexity does not guarantee performance. The contributions are: (1)~a transferable framework for rigorous ML validation in adversarial environments, (2)~detection and remediation of data leakage across multi-season backtests, (3)~honest reporting of negative results, and (4)~\textbf{practical insight that bet type selection and position-specific stability matter more than model sophistication}. This approach is applicable to any domain where validation rigor and market segmentation matter---financial trading, supply chain optimization, or resource allocation.

\chapter*{Abstract}
This dissertation presents a system-of-systems for prediction and decision-making in National Football League (NFL) betting markets, revealing \textbf{differential market efficiency across bet types}. The work documents a 13-month research journey, beginning with the catastrophic failure of monolithic predictive models (maximum drawdown $-83.5\%$) and culminating in a comprehensive data leakage investigation and nuanced performance assessment.

\textbf{Critical findings (December 2025):} Performance varies dramatically by market type. \textbf{Spread betting} shows $-7.5$\% ROI (51.0\% win rate)---confirming market efficiency for publicly available data. However, \textbf{player props} show $+2.96$\% ROI (53.89\% win rate), with high-stability positions (QB passing yards, WR receptions) likely more profitable than volatile RB metrics. Most surprisingly, a \textbf{simple power ratings model} achieved $+43$\% ROI on 2025 holdout data, significantly outperforming the sophisticated 5-way ensemble. The conclusion is nuanced: spread markets are efficient, but props markets and simple models may offer exploitable edges.

The system architecture embodies three core principles discovered through rigorous experimentation. First, a \textbf{diversified ensemble architecture} combines Bayesian (State-Space, Hierarchical), frequentist (ELO), and machine learning (XGBoost) models. Ensemble variance reduction is driven by minimizing inter-model correlation ($\bar{\rho}=0.32$) rather than maximizing individual accuracy. Second, a \textbf{hybrid static-incremental structure} (70\% static, 30\% incremental) resolves the stability-adaptation trade-off. Third, a \textbf{multi-layer validation framework} proved critical in identifying target leakage in the Ridge Residuals method (88\% ``improvement'' $\to$ 47\% degradation) and CQL bet sizing (100\% win rate $\to$ target contamination), reinforcing the importance of validation rigor over apparent performance.

The infrastructure features: idempotent data pipelines on TimescaleDB; dependence-aware pricing models (Skellam, copulas); and risk management using CVaR constraints and Kelly-LCB sizing. CQL reinforcement learning is documented but \textbf{DISABLED} pending retraining after leakage remediation. True out-of-sample testing on 2025 data achieves 74.9\% spread \emph{prediction} accuracy---but prediction accuracy does not imply betting profitability.

The contributions are methodological: (1) a complete, reproducible NFL betting system demonstrating rigorous validation practices, (2) a framework for detecting and remediating data leakage across multi-season backtests, and (3) transparent documentation of both successful and failed research paths (84-hour BNN dead end, Ridge Residuals leakage, CQL target contamination). This work provides a template for honest reporting of negative results that advance the field.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The codebase is designed for full reproducibility. All random seeds are fixed, dependencies are pinned via \texttt{requirements.txt} (Python) and \texttt{renv.lock} (R), and the README provides step-by-step instructions for replicating all results. The experiment registry tracks all model runs with hashed parameters and metrics.

\paragraph{Data Sources.}
Primary data sources include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{nflfastR}: Play-by-play data via the R package ecosystem
  \item \textbf{The Odds API}: Historical betting lines (API key required)
  \item \textbf{Meteostat}: Weather data for game conditions
  \item \textbf{NFL official data}: Schedules, rosters, and injury reports
\end{itemize}
